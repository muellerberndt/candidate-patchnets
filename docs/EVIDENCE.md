## Three distinct kinds of failure

**Numerical failure:** the returned state does not satisfy its declared
equations. The state cannot qualify merely because it moved slowly. A capped
negative control is successful as a safety test only when the cap is reported
and no unauthorized memory is committed.

**Representation/model failure:** the equations settle but the chosen model
cannot express the needed answer. The stationary scalar learner pools A and B
rather than representing distinct contexts. The binary fly transition adds
noise even though its conditional potential means are exact. Increasing solver
iterations does not repair either model mismatch.

**Capability or utility failure:** a valid model and solver may still fail to
learn a skill, retain it, generalize or meet a resource budget. Deeper observers
must earn their cost against matched alternatives. A fixed point alone does
not establish useful intelligence.

These distinctions explain why the scalar context failure does not falsify
the entire recursive-settlement hypothesis. It rules out treating that small,
stationary model as a sufficient replacement. Conversely, successful exact
inference cannot be used to claim that the larger hypothesis has been proved.
